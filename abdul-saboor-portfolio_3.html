Stage 01

Availability & inventory

Before anything can be promised, the business has to know what it actually has, where it sits, and who it belongs to.

INV-01 · built from zero

Inventory management system for a marketplace

GCC omni-channel retail marketplace · Senior PM, fulfillment technology

Situation
A marketplace lets several sellers list the same product from different locations. Treating a SKU as the unit of inventory breaks immediately.
Task
Give the platform a single inventory model that could say whose stock had just sold, and which warehouse owed the customer — built from zero.
Action
  • Defined an offer-level identity model — product, supplier and location combined into one key — so the same SKU could be listed by multiple sellers without collision.
  • Modelled stock movement as a transaction ledger: receipts, issues, transfers and adjustments, each with a reason code, rather than mutating a quantity field.
  • Set the system up as the single source of truth other systems subscribe to, instead of one more integration in a mesh.
Result
Shipped as the inventory backbone for the marketplace, adopted by every downstream service.
  • Zero → OnePlatform inventory backbone built from scratch
  • Real-timePositions replace periodic file syncs
  • Data modelling
  • Systems design
  • Marketplace
  • Zero to one

INV-02

Availability, allocation and reservation

GCC omni-channel retail marketplace · Senior PM, fulfillment technology

Situation
Overselling and stockouts are the same failure seen from two sides: the number the storefront shows is not the number the warehouse can actually pick.
Task
Converge storefront and warehouse reality in real time, while still letting the business ring-fence stock for campaigns and priority orders.
Action
  • Specified a global available-to-promise calculation covering every location and every offer, exposed in real time.
  • Added rule-based ring-fencing so stock could be held back for flash sales, priority orders or campaigns without cloning inventory records.
  • Designed a two-step reservation handshake — soft at order creation, hard on warehouse acceptance — with time and event-based release so abandoned carts return stock automatically.
Result
Storefront availability and warehouse reality converged, and campaign stock could be committed without ever being taken off sale.
  • Global ATPEvery location, every offer, live
  • 2-step reserveSoft at order, hard at acceptance
  • Real-time systems
  • ATP
  • Edge cases
  • Commercial trade-offs

INV-03

One inventory picture across five source types

GCC omni-channel retail marketplace · Senior PM, fulfillment technology

Situation
Stock lived in a third-party logistics provider, owned warehouses, retail back rooms and seller systems — each with its own schema, cadence and definition of "available".
Task
Harmonise five source types into one inventory picture, sequenced so the roadmap didn't stall on the slowest integration.
Action
  • Built an integration layer that harmonised roughly forty inventory attributes into one model, marking each field mandatory or optional by source.
  • Chose publish-subscribe over point-to-point so order management, cart and search consumed one feed rather than four.
  • Sequenced the rollout by source type, deliberately taking the third-party provider first because it was the only one already API-addressable.
Result
Unlocked fulfilled-from-store and fulfilled-by-platform models that had been blocked on inventory visibility.
  • ~40Inventory attributes harmonised
  • 5Source types, one inventory picture
  • Integration architecture
  • Phased delivery
  • Dependency management
Stage 02

Production & warehouse

The floor is where cost per order is actually won or lost — in labour minutes, walking distance, and how often something has to be done twice.

PRD-01 · flagship

Build-to-order fulfillment

European meal-kit business, three continents · Product lead, supply chain technologies

Situation
Under a build-to-stock model, every additional menu option needs its own pre-built inventory and its own share of finite line capacity.
Task
Remove the ceiling on menu size — the company's main growth lever — without a capital-expenditure fix.
Action
  • Reframed the constraint: the ceiling was in the fulfillment model, not the buildings, so the fix belonged in production software rather than capital expenditure.
  • Designed the production capability to assemble to the order instead of drawing from pre-built stock, and worked through what that changed for picking, sequencing and quality control.
  • Delivered against live sites, where the cost of getting it wrong is measured in missed customer boxes.
Result
Menu expansion decoupled from line capacity for good — production software absorbed the growth the buildings couldn't.
  • #1 objectiveAdopted company-wide, top of the roadmap
  • Top-lineDirect contribution to growth
  • Strategy
  • Constraint reframing
  • Operating model change
  • Executive alignment

PRD-02

Production control system and floor tooling

European meal-kit business, three continents · Product lead, supply chain technologies

Situation
Distribution centres on three continents run the same product on different building shapes, labour markets and volumes.
Task
Own one roadmap that works everywhere, without letting it collapse into unmaintainable per-site software.
Action
  • Own the roadmap for the production control system and the tools around it — picking applications, quality control, electronic shelf labels for replenishment and pick accuracy, and production dashboards for site leadership.
  • Hold the roadmap against four operating metrics: labour minutes per box, error rate, boxes per hour, and how far the menu can scale.
  • Draw the line between what must be global and what sites configure themselves, deliberately, rather than letting it be settled by whoever escalates loudest.
Result
A single production platform now runs across three continents, with every launch instrumented against a floor metric from day one.
  • 3Continents, one production platform
  • 4Floor metrics every launch is held to
  • Platform strategy
  • Labour efficiency
  • Global rollout
  • Operational KPIs

WMS-01 · built from zero

Omni-channel warehouse management system

GCC omni-channel retail marketplace · Senior PM, fulfillment technology

Situation
Off-the-shelf warehouse systems assume an order is fulfilled from one warehouse. This business had staked itself on orders assembled from stores, warehouses and partners at once.
Task
Decide build versus buy, then deliver the full process — receiving through returns — designed backwards from the customer's cut-off, not a demo environment.
Action
  • Made and documented the build-versus-buy case, including what buying would have bought us and why the hypothesis was too unproven to justify the price.
  • Defined the full process scope — receiving, quality check, putaway, replenishment, picking, consolidation, packing, sortation, dispatch and returns — across fulfillment centres, delivery stations, retail stores and bonded cross-border sites.
  • Planned execution backwards from the critical pull time, so the system scheduled every upstream step against the cut-off and flagged delays before they reached the customer.
  • Cut hardware cost by specifying consumer devices with clip-on infrared scanners in place of industrial handhelds.
Result
Shipped as a mobile app for the floor and a web console for warehouse managers, API-first so third-party hardware and software could attach.
  • 1.5K/dayOrders at design point
  • 500KItems in storage
  • 99.5%Delivered in full & on time
  • <24hFulfillment turnaround
  • Build vs buy
  • WMS
  • Process design
  • Hardware strategy
  • Chain of custody

Stage 03

Promise & planning

The delivery date shown at checkout is the most consequential number in e-commerce. It sets the customer's expectation and commits the network to meeting it.

PRM-01

Dynamic delivery promise

Omnichannel retail group, MENA · Senior PM, e-commerce supply chain

Situation
A fixed delivery promise is wrong twice over: too cautious for customers near a stocked warehouse, too optimistic for everyone else.
Task
Build the promise from live signals — stock location, fulfilling site, reachable cut-off, customer location — instead of a static table.
Action
  • Built the promise from real signals — where the stock is, which site can fulfil, the cut-off still reachable, and the customer's actual location — rather than a static table.
  • Added location intelligence to improve address accuracy in a region where addressing is genuinely hard.
Result
The promise shown at checkout finally matched what the network could actually deliver.
  • +14%Same/next-day availability
  • -4.7%Failed deliveries
  • +0.7NPS
  • Delivery promise
  • Location intelligence
  • Conversion
  • Cost to serve
